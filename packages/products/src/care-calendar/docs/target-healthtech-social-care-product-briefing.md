# Target Healthtech and Social-Care Companies: Product-Level Briefing

This is a lightweight first-call briefing for Tier 1 and Tier 2 target companies.

The aim is not to memorise every feature. For each company, be able to:

1. Name one or two core products.
2. Identify the primary users.
3. Explain the workflow each product supports.
4. Articulate the underlying problem.
5. Ask one thoughtful product question.

Some companies sell a clearly named suite of products. Others sell a configurable platform or a technology-enabled care service. Sounding credible sometimes means recognising that difference rather than forcing everything into a conventional product catalogue.

# Tier 1

## Patients Know Best

**Domain:** Healthcare with cross-boundary health and social-care information sharing  
**Fit:** 9.5/10

### Product offering

Patients Know Best primarily offers one integrated product: its **Personal Health Record**, sometimes described as a single patient record.

Within that record, patients can use:

- **Health Record:** Results, diagnoses, medications, allergies, clinical documents and information received from connected providers.
- **Care Plans:** Plans that patients and professionals can review and contribute to.
- **Messages:** Secure communication involving patients, professionals and authorised carers.
- **Appointments:** Visibility of scheduled care.
- **Measurements and device data:** Information entered manually or received from connected health apps and devices.
- **Sharing controls:** The patient decides which professionals, relatives or carers can access relevant parts of the record.
- **NHS App integration:** Patients can access parts of their PKB record without treating PKB as a completely separate destination.

PKB has also been developing a **Population Health Management engine**, using information in the patient-controlled record to identify relevant services, clinical trials or interventions. The strategically important point is that this builds on the shared longitudinal record rather than creating another disconnected dataset.

### Problems it solves

The product addresses several forms of fragmentation:

- Patients receiving information through separate hospital portals, letters and conversations.
- Professionals lacking visibility of information held elsewhere.
- Patients and carers repeatedly reconstructing medical history.
- Information being technically available but not available to the person whose care it concerns.
- Limited continuity when someone moves between organisations or care settings.

### Credible call articulation

> I understand PKB primarily as a patient-controlled longitudinal record rather than simply a patient portal. The distinction that matters to me is that the record can bring together information from multiple organisations while letting the patient share it with professionals and carers. That addresses continuity and agency, not just digital access.

### Product question worth asking

How do product teams help patients **understand** a large longitudinal record, rather than merely making more information visible?

## Birdie

**Domain:** Domiciliary social care  
**Fit:** 9/10

### Product offering

Birdie presents its product as one connected home-care platform with two primary interfaces.

#### Agency Hub

The web-based command centre for office staff, managers and care coordinators. It covers:

- Digital care planning and assessments.
- Client records and risk information.
- Rostering and scheduling.
- Workforce availability and matching.
- Finance, invoicing and payroll-related workflows.
- Auditing, compliance and incident oversight.
- Reporting and business performance.

#### Carer App

The mobile interface used during care delivery. It gives care professionals access to:

- Visit schedules and client information.
- Care plans and tasks.
- Point-of-care notes.
- Medication information and electronic medication administration records.
- Body maps and observations.
- Alerts and changes relevant to a visit.

Birdie also offers **Birdie Analytics/Pulse dashboards**, which bring together care, workforce and financial measures for managers.

Its functional product areas are commonly described as:

- **Care Management**
- **Rostering**
- **Finance**
- **Auditing and Compliance**
- **Workforce Management**
- **Business Visibility**

### Problems it solves

Birdie is attempting to replace a fragmented operating model in which:

- Care plans live in one system or on paper.
- Rotas are managed separately.
- Carers rely on calls or messages for updates.
- Medication records are disconnected from the rest of care delivery.
- Finance teams recreate information already recorded elsewhere.
- Managers only discover delivery problems after the fact.

The platform’s value is not one individual feature. It is the connection between the scheduled visit, the person’s care plan, what the carer records and what the agency can see operationally.

### Credible call articulation

> I see Birdie as more than a digital care-record product. The Agency Hub and Carer App connect planning, rostering, care delivery, medication, compliance and finance. The interesting product challenge is making those connected workflows feel coherent to people with very different jobs.

### Product question worth asking

Do engineering teams own complete journeys spanning the Agency Hub and Carer App, or are the two experiences developed mainly as separate product areas?

## Accurx

**Domain:** Healthcare communication infrastructure  
**Fit:** 9/10

### Product offering

Accurx has evolved from a GP messaging tool into a broader communication platform used across primary, community, mental-health and secondary care.

Its prominent products and capabilities include:

#### Patient Triage

Patients submit structured clinical or administrative requests to their GP practice. Teams can review, categorise, assign and respond without every request beginning as a phone call or appointment.

#### Message Patient and Batch Messaging

Healthcare teams send SMS, email or NHS App messages to individuals or patient groups, including documents, links and requests for information.

#### Florey questionnaires and forms

Structured questionnaires used to gather information from patients remotely—for example, symptoms, measurements or follow-up information.

#### Self-Book and appointment communication

Patients receive links to choose from available appointments, while reminders and follow-up messages reduce manual booking work.

#### Message GP and professional-to-professional communication

Hospital and community professionals can communicate with GP practices and other services about patient care.

#### Accurx Inbox and Patient Portal

Professionals manage incoming communication, while patients can see messages, documents and some appointment details in a patient-facing portal.

#### Scribe

An AI medical scribe that captures consultations and drafts structured notes, with the aim of reducing typing during clinical encounters.

#### Navigator and newer workflow automation

Accurx is developing more intelligent categorisation, summaries and assistance for handling demand.

### Problems it solves

Accurx addresses communication failures such as:

- Patients being unable to get through by phone.
- Every request being forced into an appointment-shaped workflow.
- Staff manually sending the same information repeatedly.
- Hospitals and GPs relying on slow or informal communication.
- Patient-provided information being collected inconsistently.
- Clinicians dividing attention between the patient and documentation.

### Credible call articulation

> I understand Accurx as a set of communication primitives used across care: triage, structured forms, messaging, self-booking, professional-to-professional communication and now Scribe. What makes the platform interesting is how those capabilities can be composed into different workflows without losing clarity for patients or staff.

### Product question worth asking

As the suite expands, how does Accurx prevent the patient experience from feeling like a collection of separate messages, forms and links?

## Isla Health

**Domain:** Healthcare digital pathways and remote monitoring  
**Fit:** 8.5/10

### Product offering

Isla’s main product is its **Digital Pathway Platform**. It is a configurable, UKCA-marked Class I medical device rather than a collection of separately branded applications.

Healthcare teams use it to build pathways involving:

- Digital referral and triage.
- Patient-submitted photographs, video and structured forms.
- Remote monitoring over time.
- Asynchronous clinical review.
- Automated reminders and routine workflow steps.
- Follow-up and discharge.
- Escalation or onward action when information requires review.
- Integration with systems including EMIS, Epic, Cerner, SystmOne and Rio.

Typical pathway examples include wound monitoring, dermatology, post-operative review and other services where clinicians can safely assess progress from structured information or media without every interaction requiring an in-person appointment.

### Problems it solves

Isla addresses:

- Information arriving through email, messaging apps or other unstructured channels.
- Unnecessary appointments for routine review.
- Clinicians manually chasing patients for photographs or updates.
- Pathway information being scattered across separate systems.
- Inconsistent follow-up.
- Clinical capacity being consumed by routine administration rather than judgment.

### Credible call articulation

> I understand Isla as a configurable digital-pathway platform, not simply an image-upload product. Images and patient-reported information are inputs into pathways covering referral, triage, monitoring, review and discharge. The product challenge is keeping those pathways safe and understandable while accommodating very different specialties.

### Product question worth asking

What is standard platform capability, and what is configured or built specifically for an individual specialty or NHS trust?

## Cera

**Domain:** Technology-enabled healthcare and social-care delivery  
**Fit:** 8/10

### Product offering

Cera is different from Birdie or Nourish because it does not primarily sell a care-management SaaS product to external agencies. It is a care provider using its own technology to operate and improve the service it delivers.

Its key product is the **Cera App**, used by its frontline carers and nurses to:

- View visits and care tasks.
- Access information about the person receiving care.
- Record care and observations.
- Capture data during visits.
- Identify and communicate changes.

Cera then uses the operational data collected through its platform for machine-learning models intended to identify risks such as deterioration or falls.

This technology supports Cera’s actual service lines:

- Elderly care at home.
- Nursing and complex care.
- Live-in care.
- Supported living.
- Extra care.
- Learning-disability services.

### Problems it solves

Cera is addressing both service and software problems:

- Shortages and inefficiency in home-care delivery.
- Carers relying on paper or disconnected information.
- Deterioration being identified only after a crisis.
- Avoidable hospitalisation.
- Operational systems that record completed care but do little to support prevention.

### Credible call articulation

> I understand Cera as a vertically integrated care provider rather than a conventional software vendor. The Cera App supports its own carers and nurses, and the resulting delivery data powers its preventative-care models. That creates a much tighter feedback loop between product decisions and real care delivery.

### Product question worth asking

How directly can engineers observe frontline use and distinguish a model-generated risk from something clinically or operationally actionable?

#### Autism Assessment Pathways

NHS-funded assessments through Right to Choose, involving:

- GP referral.
- Parent or carer information.
- Developmental history.
- Clinical assessment and observation.
- Multidisciplinary review.
- Diagnostic outcome and report.
- Recommendations for home and education.

Some pathways are remote; others include an in-person observation.

#### ADHD Assessment Pathways

Structured assessment using:

- Referral information.
- Family and school evidence.
- Clinical interviews.
- Diagnostic review.
- A written report and recommendations.

Where appropriate, the service may discuss management options, including medication pathways.

#### Combined referral routes

GPs can refer children for autism, ADHD or both where appropriate.

#### Family and GP digital journeys

The technology opportunity is likely in referral, evidence collection, appointment coordination, communication and making a long assessment process transparent to families and clinicians.

### Problems it solves

Paloma addresses:

- Long waits for neurodevelopmental assessment.
- Families not understanding referral and eligibility routes.
- Evidence being gathered inconsistently from families and schools.
- Multidisciplinary assessment requiring substantial coordination.
- Families receiving little visibility while waiting.
- Delays preventing children from receiving appropriate educational and practical support.

### Credible call articulation

> Paloma is currently a focused clinical service rather than a broad health platform. The product opportunity sits around the end-to-end autism and ADHD assessment journey: referral, evidence gathering, parent and school input, appointments, multidisciplinary review and a clear outcome.

### Product question worth asking

Is the long-term ambition primarily to scale Paloma’s own clinical service, or to develop reusable technology that could support other assessment providers?

## Huma

**Domain:** Regulated digital-health platforms and disease-specific products  
**Fit:** 7.5/10

### Product offering

Huma has two layers: a configurable platform and specific digital-health products.

#### Huma Cloud Platform

A regulated infrastructure for building:

- Remote patient-monitoring programmes.
- Virtual wards.
- Disease-management applications.
- Patient companion apps.
- Risk-assessment tools.
- Dose calculators.
- Predictive algorithms.
- Clinician dashboards and review workflows.

#### Huma Intelligence

AI and analytical capabilities that can operate across collected patient information and digital pathways.

Named condition or setting-specific products include:

#### Respiratory

Remote lung-health monitoring using patient-generated measurements and symptoms.

#### A&E eTriage

Digital triage for emergency and urgent-care settings.

#### GDm-Health

A digital programme for gestational diabetes, connecting patient-recorded glucose information with clinical review.

#### Huma MG

A medical-grade application and remote-monitoring platform for myasthenia gravis.

#### Virtual Wards and Remote Monitoring

Configurable programmes for monitoring people outside hospital and identifying issues earlier.

### Problems it solves

Huma targets:

- Every digital-health programme being built from scratch.
- Patient data only being available during appointments.
- Clinicians manually reviewing disconnected device and questionnaire data.
- Regulated product development taking too long.
- Disease-specific applications lacking reusable infrastructure.
- Patients with long-term conditions receiving episodic rather than continuous support.

### Credible call articulation

> Huma operates at both platform and product levels. The Cloud Platform supplies regulated remote-monitoring and disease-management capabilities, while products such as GDm-Health, A&E eTriage and Huma MG apply those capabilities to specific clinical problems. I’d want to understand whether a role sat close to one patient journey or primarily within the platform layer.

### Product question worth asking

How much autonomy do disease-product teams have relative to the shared Huma Cloud Platform, particularly over frontend experience and interaction design?

# Using this briefing in a call

A strong answer usually needs:

1. **One product name**
2. **Its primary users**
3. **The workflow it supports**
4. **The underlying problem**
5. **One thoughtful question**

For example:

> I’ve been looking at Nourish Better Care and Better Care at Home. My understanding is that both cover care planning and point-of-care recording, but the home-care product also has to deal with mobile visits, connectivity and coordination across a distributed workforce. I’d be interested in how much architecture they share and how much the home-care experience is treated as a distinct product.

That sounds more credible than either repeating the company mission or naming ten features without understanding how they connect.
